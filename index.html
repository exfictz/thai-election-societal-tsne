2026 Thai general election — which societal factors predict the winning party?

The left window is a geographic map of the 77 province centroids, coloured by the party that won the most raw votes (masked from the embedding). The right window starts as the same map; select factors on the left and press Calculate t-SNE — the points animate from their geographic positions into the t-SNE embedding computed from only the selected factors (region one-hot encoded). Homogeneous single-party clusters mean the chosen factors are good predictors; mixed clusters mean try other factors. Pure client-side web app — no server needed.

Result: the party with the most raw votes (combined party-list count) per province in the 2026 Thai general election; it is never an input to the embedding.
Factors: province-level turnout (1 − turnout), invalid party-list / constituency ballot rates, household internet, median equivalised monthly household income, poverty headcount ratio & poverty %, equivalised income Gini, household debt-to-monthly-income ratio, employment YoY change, agriculture & manufacturing GPP shares, urban population %, population 60+ % and population change, skip-generation households %, higher education (25+) %, non-formal education students %, no-higher-education rate; region is one-hot encoded (กลาง / ตะวันออกเฉียงเหนือ / เหนือ / ใต้) before being fed to t-SNE. Province centroids for the map view come from polygon-derived centroid data.
Embedding: t-SNE (tsne.js, bundled locally) on standardized selected factors; the winning party is never an input. Hover a point for details; the map view uses province centroids (equirectangular projection).
